# Obstruction Removal

## Decision rule

Removal is editorial, not cosmetic. Remove only when an element blocks the chosen subject more than it contributes to evidence or story.

Ask:

1. Does it cover a unique subject anchor?
2. Does it dominate by saturation, scale, contrast, or direction?
3. Does it add scale, use, culture, time, or human presence?
4. Can hidden geometry be inferred without pretending to recover truth?
5. Will removal make the scene implausibly clean?

## Two-stage requirement

Use a separate clean-base edit when the obstruction covers more than a small local patch, intersects repeated structures, or hides subject geometry.

### Stage A — documentary base

Prompt for removal only. Lock crop, perspective, weather, color, subject positions, and all non-target objects. Reconstruct conservatively:

- continue only structures established above/below the obstruction;
- sample nearby texture and irregularity;
- preserve natural clutter;
- do not invent detailed motifs, faces, signs, text, or new objects;
- do not beautify, relight, or stylize.

### Stage A acceptance

Reject if there are:

- duplicated or melted objects;
- repeated vegetation stamps;
- floating fragments;
- suspiciously empty or over-clean bands;
- changed subject count or identity;
- inconsistent road, curb, wall, trunk, or horizon geometry;
- residual shadows or supports from the removed object.

Disclose that occluded content is inferred, not restored.

### Stage B — material artwork

Use the accepted clean base as input. Re-lock reconstructed geometry. Never ask the material treatment to hide Stage A defects.

## Preserve useful imperfection

Do not remove road edges, weeds, wires, tools, passersby, puddles, or signs merely because they are untidy. Preserve them when they establish reality, scale, activity, place, or the physical bridge between media.
