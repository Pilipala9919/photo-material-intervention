# Recipe: Paint-Stop Charcoal

## Eligibility

Use when real paint, coating, markings, or human-applied color visibly stops on a subject and unpainted form continues into fog, darkness, smoke, or low-detail atmosphere.

If a foreground structure blocks the painted subject and has low narrative value, run the clean-base workflow first.

## Event

> The human-painted section stays real; above the last brushstroke, the subject and atmosphere continue as charcoal.

## Locks

Preserve:

- exact painted sections, colors, patterns, wear, and texture;
- position, width, species, count, and perspective;
- lower environment that establishes documentary reality.

Never invent detailed hidden motifs. Reconstructed occlusions must be disclosed.

## Intervention

Begin independently at each real paint endpoint—never a horizontal split. Continue unpainted structure and atmospheric background with:

- compressed charcoal mass;
- vine-charcoal structure;
- side-of-stick rubbing;
- finger smudge and dust;
- kneaded-erased fog/light;
- broken, incomplete edges.

Recommended intervention: 45–65%. Keep applied color as the only strong color anchor.

## Contact

Charcoal catches in bark/material grooves; microscopic source pigment enters the first few centimeters of monochrome; real vegetation overlaps the lowest drawing.

## Failure modes

- whole-image pencil filter;
- incorrect species;
- colored crowns or rainbow extension;
- all boundaries at one height;
- clean white drawing paper replaces real fog;
- obstruction removal leaves repeated or melted structure.

## Motion

First reveal the clean subject if obstruction was removed. Then let charcoal rise from each paint endpoint at unequal timing; finish by lifting fog with an eraser.
