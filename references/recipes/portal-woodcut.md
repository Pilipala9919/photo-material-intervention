# Recipe: Portal Woodcut

## Eligibility

Use when a real doorway, window, arch, tunnel, mirror, or opening creates a strong boundary and a subject observes, enters, or stands across it.

Do not use for ordinary landscapes without a meaningful portal.

## Event

> A truthful photographic observer faces a world inside the existing opening that has become a hand-pulled relief print.

## Locks

Preserve:

- observer identity, posture, clothing, and scale;
- portal geometry;
- important foreground overlaps;
- exact exterior/interior spatial relationships.

## Intervention

Transform only the source-defined world through the opening. Preserve recognizable structure with relief language:

- carved channels and gouges;
- one dark key plate plus one or two source-derived color plates;
- broken ink pressure and unprinted cuts;
- reduced but faithful scene geometry.

Recommended intervention: 40–70%, with observer and portal photographic.

## Contact

Use existing geometry: vines cross the print, silhouette overlaps it, reflection receives carved marks, wet ink leaks only at the threshold. Avoid an isolated pasted rectangle.

## Failure modes

- clouds become waves or invented scenery;
- exterior is merely posterized;
- person is redrawn;
- portal becomes an added frame;
- full scene becomes illustration;
- contact is absent.

## Motion

Carve the transformed world in layers while the observer remains still. End with one contact detail—reflection or threshold ink—rather than a slogan.
